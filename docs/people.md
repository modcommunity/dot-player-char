# People

Why the family's players, bots and NPCs are drawn the way `DotHumanoidFigure` draws them, and why the obvious alternative is wrong in each case. The classes are `DotHumanoidLook` (what somebody looks like, as data), `DotHumanoidClips` (the clip set, retargeting, the made clips) and `DotHumanoidFigure` (the drawn person). The files are in `addons/dot_player_char/people/`, built by `tools/build_people.py`.

## One style for everybody, from one rig

Before this, each game drew its people its own way, and most drew Kenney's Blocky Characters: one 2.7 m body with eighteen painted skins, its animation node transforms on six rigid parts. game-zombie-zone then adopted animated skinned zombies, and its survivors (still blocks) stopped matching the zombies chasing them in every frame. That is the play test's complaint, and the family's answer is one rig for everybody.

The people are the four survivors of Quaternius' [Zombie Apocalypse Kit](https://quaternius.com/packs/zombieapocalypsekit.html) (CC0 1.0): `matt`, `lis`, `sam`, `shaun`. The reasons for this kit over the alternatives:

- **The zombies are already on it.** game-zombie-zone's zombies are the same kit's zombies, on the same rig with the same bone names, so a survivor and the thing chasing them are one style, and one ragdoll and one clip set serve both.
- **It is animated, and the clips are the family's list.** Idle, walk, run, the three again holding a gun, jump, fall, land, slash, stab, punch, a hit, a death, a dodge, a wave, yes and no. The zombie's crawl is the same rig, so a downed player crawls with it.
- **Kenney's kits are not.** Blocky Characters is rigid parts and Mini Characters has no skeleton; neither can bend a knee, which is what a crouch and a crawl are.
- Quaternius' Ultimate Animated Character Pack (also CC0, also downloaded and compared) has more costumes but thinner, smaller-headed bodies on a 23-bone rig: beside the kit's zombies it reads as a second art style, which is the problem being fixed.

Kenney's assets stay first everywhere else; for animated people there is no Kenney kit that does the job.

## Variety is colour, not texture

The four bodies share one atlas of flat swatches. A look is a body and four colours (skin, hair, top, bottom), and the figure's palette shader swaps each body's own swatches for the look's (`WEARS`, measured by `tools/people_palette.gd`). Four bodies times any colours is enough for a server of strangers to look different, a side's colour goes on the top (`with_side`), and a member's site avatar becomes colours too.

- **One material per look, no per-instance uniforms.** A look's material is built once and shared by everybody who wears it (`material_for`, keyed by `key()`), the choice game-zombie-zone's palette shader made for the compatibility renderer (every browser); a crowd of forty is a handful of materials.
- **The swap is matched in both colour spaces**, and answered in the one it matched: what a `source_color` sampler hands back differs by renderer, and game-zombie-zone's first palette shader, converting by hand, drew every zombie near black on one of them.
- **Colour arrays are `vec4`**: a `vec3` array is padded on the GPU, and a packed `Vector3` array set into one is read skewed.

## The site's avatar

The site's built-in avatar is Kenney's blocky kit: three slots (face, top, legs), each one of the eighteen skins `skin-a` to `skin-r`. `DotHumanoidLook.from_site` turns one into a person: the face's letter picks the body and gives the skin and hair colour, the top's and the legs' give the clothes. `SITE_SKINS` is the colour under the most area of each part of each skin, measured by `tools/kenney_site_colours.py`. A member who dressed as the robot is grey and red here; the person is recognisable without the block body.

It is duck-typed (`schema_id`, `part_in`), so this addon does not depend on dot-user-avatar, and it reads the site's `<slot>.<option>` part ids as well as bare ones: checking only the bare form once refused every real site avatar in six games.

## One clip set, carried to every body

Only `people_matt.glb` carries clips; the others are a quarter of the size without them. `DotHumanoidClips.retarget` carries the set onto each other body once and every figure of that body shares it.

**A clip stores where each bone is, not how far it moved.** Two bodies on one rig still differ in rest pose (here, at the shoulder and the head), and a clip played as it is on another body bends it. Each key is taken off the clip body's rest and put on the target's: rotation `to * from^-1 * key`, a position by the same turn about the new rest.

### The made clips

- **The crouch and the crouch walk are made, not drawn.** The kit has no crouch. Its duck is a dodge with both arms over the head; held at its lowest it reads as cowering. `synthesise` adds the duck's bend at its lowest frame (as a change from the idle pose, on the body and legs) to the idle and to the walk, so the idle's breathing and the walk's stride stay. A crouched player who stands up to walk reads as not crouching, which is the one thing a crouch has to say.
- **Sitting is a real clip, with a made one under it** ([below](#real-clips-from-the-universal-animation-library)). The made sit (`_sat`, from the idle's first frame: the thighs laid forward level, the shins hanging straight down, the feet flat under the knees, the body dropped until they are on the floor) is what a figure plays when `people_ual.res` is missing. A figure with `seated` in its motion plays the sit whatever speed it is carried at, and `seat_height()` (about 0.28 m at 1.8 m: these are short cartoon legs) is how far under the seat a game puts the figure's origin. A rider drawn crouching, the stand-in before both, was somebody squatting over the cushion.
- **Climbing is made from the idle too** (`_climbing`): one knee lifted to the next rung while the other leg straightens, the hands over the head with the one on the straight leg's side higher, one second a cycle and played as fast as the player climbs. The body is pushed up until the lower foot is where the feet stood (the idle stands on bent knees, and a straight leg would put its foot through the origin), which is also the pull-up. The crawl it played before was somebody lying on their front, drawn upright on a ladder.
- **Swimming is a made front crawl** (`_swimming`), drawn upright and laid forward about the hips by the figure (`SWIM_LEAN`, about 80 degrees), so in the clip "up" is ahead of the swimmer, "front" is down into the water and "back" is up out of it. Each arm goes round the body's side half a turn from the other: the reach, a bent-elbow pull under the chest, the finish at the thigh, and a recovery swung wide and low with the hand hanging towards the water. The first try was a windmill in the body's plane, and a recovering arm stood straight up like a mast. The body rolls towards the pulling arm, the head turns to breathe on the right once a stroke, and the legs flutter three kicks to a stroke with the toes pointed. One stroke is `SWIM_CYCLE` (1.6 s), played faster with speed. A swimmer holding a gun does not get the held-gun arms: the stroke is the arms. The stand-in before was the run, slowed and leaned 70 degrees. Looked at in `people_sheet.gd --swim` (side, ahead and above, behind and above, with a water surface at the hips).
- **The feet hang off the root on this rig** (for the kit's own leg IK, which nothing here runs), not off the shins. A made pose that moves a leg moves its foot to where the shin now ends, and measures the shin before it moves the body: the first sit took it after the drop, so the shin was a quarter shorter and the feet floated.
- **The jump, the fall and the landing are grounded.** The kit's are in-place jumps that lift the body (and the feet, which hang off the root in this rig) in the clip. A player in the air is already lifted by the game, so drawn as it is a falling player hung half a metre above their own capsule. The rise is taken out at every key; the landing's squat is kept.

### Not in the kit

- **A figure something else carries** (an NPC on a physics body) sets `auto_animate`: it measures how fast it moves and animates from that. The body moves in physics ticks (or snapshots), so at a frame rate above the tick rate it stands still on most frames: the speed is measured over at least `AUTO_WINDOW` (0.05 s) of moves, held through `AUTO_HOLD` (0.1 s) without one, and the figure is posed on the frames its body stepped (at least every `AUTO_POSE_GAP`, 1/60 s, standing). Measured frame to frame, game-playground's NPCs at 355 frames a second against 128 ticks flipped idle, walk and run 218 times a second each, every flip a crossfade and a stride match.
- **A real ladder climb** would be better than the made one above; no free CC0 clip has one (see the next section). Nor does any have a seat with the arms on armrests: the real sit has the hands in the lap.

## Real clips from the Universal Animation Library

Quaternius' [Universal Animation Library](https://quaternius.com/packs/universalanimationlibrary.html) (CC0 1.0) is the same artist's clip library, on a different skeleton: a 65-bone mannequin about 1.8 tall, with a hand bone, three-joint fingers and the feet under the shins, where the kit has 43 bones, is about 1.1 tall, and hangs its feet off the root. `tools/ual_retarget.gd` carries a clip across and saves it into `people/people_ual.res`, and `DotHumanoidClips.adopt` puts each clip in that file over the made one of the same name, after the mirror and the made clips (the library is right-handed already).

- **Turned in skeleton space, not copied locally.** Both rigs rest in a T pose facing +Z, so each kit bone gets how far its mapped bone turned from rest in the skeleton's space (`turn = ual_global * ual_rest_global^-1`, then `turn * kit_rest_global`). Local rotations cannot be copied: the bones' own axes differ. Bone lengths stay the kit's; the hips' travel is scaled by the ratio of hip heights; a foot is put where its shin now ends (as the made clips do); a grounded clip is then moved until its lower foot is where the rest pose's feet are.
- **The bone map** (`MAP`): `Body` is the pelvis and `Hips`, `Abdomen`, `Torso` the three spine bones; the kit's `Middle1`, `Index1` and `Pinky1` all start at the wrist and are the palm, so they follow the hand, and their second and third joints follow the first two finger joints.
- **The free version has no ladder climb.** Standard (free, 43 clips) has sitting, crouching, swimming, driving and pistol aims; climbing is only in the paid Pro and Source versions, so it is not used. The second library's free Standard has `ClimbUp_1m`, a mantle onto a ledge, which is not a ladder.
- **What was compared and kept**, each rendered beside the made clip with `people_sheet.gd --compare` (the file's clips, made and real, three-quarter and side) and looked at:
  - `sit` from `Sitting_Idle_Loop`: **kept.** Upright, hands resting on the thighs, breathing; the made sit held both arms out over the knees as if reaching for something.
  - `crouch` from `Crouch_Idle_Loop` and `crouch_walk` from `Crouch_Fwd_Loop`: **not kept.** A deep squat with the head down and the knuckles on the floor: it reads as hiding, not as a player crouched and looking ahead, and the hands on the floor fight the held-gun arms. The made crouch stays.
  - `swim` from `Swim_Fwd_Loop` (stood up about the hips with `^`, since the figure lays the swim down itself): **not kept.** A head-up stroke whose arms swing out level to the sides at one point of the cycle; not clearly better than the made crawl, which was tuned frame by frame.
  - `survivor_compare.gd` (made left, real right, same share of the cycle, side camera) for run/jog/sprint, walk/jog, hit chest/head and death/knockback: **none kept.** The real run and jog are not clearly better than the made ones and the hit and knockback clips shift the hips off the capsule; only the clips already in `people_ual.res` could be compared (no real walk, idle or death is baked).
  - **Walk, idle and death (UAL1 `Walk_Loop`, `Idle_Loop`, `Death01`, baked as `ual_walk`, `ual_idle`, `ual_death`; `survivor_compare.gd` rows 6 to 8, side view): none replace the made clip, all three are baked for games to play by name.** `ual_walk` is a calm upright walk, feet clean (lowest foot floats 0.005 m against the made walk's 0.063) but its stride is short: the planted foot moves at 0.53 m/s against the made walk's 1.47, so at the same ground speed it would need about 2.8 times the playback rate and still looks like a stroll; the made walk stays the survivor's. `ual_idle` (2.5 s) is a gentle breathing stand, near identical to the made idle; no gain. `ual_death` falls back onto the back in 2.4 s and lies flat, readable, but the hips go 0.079 m below the floor (`free` mode keeps the clip's own hip height) and it is 3 times slower than the made death; the ragdoll deaths are what games use, so it is left unplayed. Not looked at in a game. Sprint, jog and crouch-walk rows are unchanged from before. Crouch-walk foot metrics (floats 0.009 for the real clip, 0.063 for the made) only measure lowest-foot height and are blind to knee bend and hip depth, so they cannot say the made crouch walk's foot lift is enough; that needs a look at the strip.
  - The made `crouch_walk` was too deep and barely changed over the cycle; it now bends at `CROUCH_WALK_BEND` (0.7 of the crouch) with a two-dip hip bob (`CROUCH_WALK_BOB`).
  - `Driving_Loop` (hands on a wheel) and `Sitting_Talking_Loop` read well but have no made clip to replace and no motion that asks for them; a driver pose would need a `driving` motion from the games (queue item `people-drive-clip-1`).
- **The slide** plays when the game sends `sliding` in `set_motion` while grounded (`DotHumanoidClips.SLIDE`, `ual_slide`); the clip's hips sit below the floor, so the figure lifts the model `SLIDE_LIFT` and blends 0.35 s in and out (`SLIDE_BLEND`). A mirrored player's slide is not replicated: game-fatal-contact infers it from a crouch at running speed. Look at it with `tools/shot_stance.sh <tag>` (stand, run, crouch, crouch walk, slide, stand; prints the worst head step per frame).
- **The `ual_*` clips (both libraries, free Standard, CC0)** are baked beside `sit` for games to play by name: `ual_zombie_idle`, `ual_zombie_walk`, `ual_zombie_scratch`, `ual_sprint`, `ual_jog`, `ual_crouch_walk`, `ual_hit_chest`, `ual_hit_head`, `ual_walk`, `ual_idle` (grounded) and `ual_knockback`, `ual_lay_to_idle`, `ual_slide`, `ual_death` (free: the clip's own hip height is kept). The free tier has only three zombie clips: no zombie run, crawl or lay-face-up; the paid tiers add them. game-zombie-zone's commons play them (`ZzZombieView.UAL_FOR`), carried from matt's rest onto each zombie body's, and fall back to the made clips when the file is missing.
- **`zombie_gait`** on the figure plays `ual_zombie_idle`, `ual_zombie_walk` (rate from speed over 1.3 m/s) and the real sprint for an idle, a walk and a run, and the person's own clips for everything else; game-airdrift's hunters use it with `auto_animate`. Looked at in no rendered strip yet.
- **Cost:** `people_ual.res` is 229 KB (16 clips at 15 keys a second, `ual_sprint` at 60 so its short foot plant is not sampled away, still tracks kept to one key, compressed). It lives in the addon, so it ships with any game's pack that ships the addon.

## Aiming up and down

`aim(pitch_radians)` (up positive; a first-person controller's `state.pitch` is degrees) bends the upper body after the clip and the overlays: the waist and chest a little under a quarter of the pitch, the neck a quarter more so the head reads as looking there, and the weapon arm the rest at the shoulder when the hand holds something, so the gun points the whole way (`AIM_BONES`, `AIM_ARM`). The other arm hangs as the clip has it. Held to about 66 degrees; nothing while down, dead, swimming or climbing. Looked at in `people_sheet.gd --aim` (standing, front, crouched and seated, from straight down to straight up).

- **Most of the pitch goes to the arm, not the spine.** The first try put it all in the waist and chest: on a cartoon-proportioned body a player looking down was bowing and one looking up was falling over backwards.
- **The parents' poses come from the local poses up the chain**, not `get_bone_global_pose`, which is last frame's until the skeleton updates (with last frame's bend in it). Each bone's share goes on as `P^-1 * turn * P * local` with every `P` read before anything turns: turns about one axis commute.
- **Only on a bone the playing clip keys.** A bone no track writes keeps last frame's pose, and a bend put on it would add up frame after frame.
- A game passes it as `"aim"` in `set_motion` (left out, it stays). A remote player is drawn aiming only if the game sends the pitch; the games' snapshots already carry it for the view.

## Emotes, poses and gestures

`DotHumanoidFigure.play_action(id, loop, elapsed)` plays one of dot-user-avatar's 13 starter ids (`DotHumanoidActions`): `wave` and `nod` are the kit's own clips, `sit` the real sit, the other ten are made from bone directions like the climb (no art vendored). `sit` and `dance` are the whole body and play only while standing still; every other id is the upper body over the legs, through the overlay machinery, eased in and out. A pose is a looping clip the figure holds (`action` stays set) until `stop_action()`, a jump, or the figure moving off; a gesture or emote plays once. `elapsed` joins a clip already running, for a player seen late.

- **`action` is set after `_start_overlay`**, which clears it.
- **Uploaded clips** arrive as dot-user-avatar's cached scene and go through `DotHumanoidFigure.add_action_scene(content_id, scene, clip, loop)`: `DotHumanoidActions.from_upload` guesses a humanoid `BoneMap` from the source's bone names (`profile_bone_of`: Mixamo, the profile's own, `.L`/`_R`), keeps only `KIT_BONES`, re-paths the tracks to this body's skeleton, retargets with `DotHumanoidClips.retarget`, and refuses a clip over 30 s or moving fewer than 4 kit bones. The body's library is shared, so every figure of that body gets it; `clip_name` turns the id's slashes into underscores. dot-game's `DotGameAvatarActionClient` calls it for other players' uploads. A T-pose source onto this A-pose rig is still rough. Contact sheet: `people_sheet.gd --actions`.

## Legs from the clip, arms from an overlay

A swing, a flinch and a gesture are one-shots that would stop the legs if played as the clip. The figure advances its `AnimationPlayer` by hand (`ANIMATION_CALLBACK_MODE_PROCESS_MANUAL`) and then writes the upper body (`UPPER_BONES`) from the overlay, blended in and out, so a player swinging while running keeps running. Holding a gun in a clip that has no gun version (a crouch, the air) writes the held-gun arms the same way. Left to the engine, the clip and the overlay run in an order nothing controls, and an arm written before the clip is an arm the clip puts back.

**The gun hand is the right, because the clips are mirrored.** The kit's people are left-handed: every weapon in it hangs off `Middle1.L`, and its gun and melee clips hold them there. The family's first-person view models are right-handed, so everybody else saw a player hold the gun in the other hand from the one they saw it in. `DotHumanoidClips.mirror` turns the whole set left for right once, when it is loaded (before the made clips and the retarget): each `.L` track becomes the `.R` bone's and every key is reflected across the middle (a rotation's y and z negated, a position's x). That is exact because the rig is symmetric (each `.R` rest is the `.L` rest reflected, measured to the thousandth). The whole set rather than the gun clips alone, so a swing, a punch and a wave are the same hand as the gun. `attachment("right_hand")` (the family's mount name for "where a weapon goes", which `ZeeWorldModel` asks for) is `Middle1.R`. The attachment's frame is worked out from the held-gun pose: forward is the figure's forward and up is up there, at metre scale whatever the body's, so a weapon model made pointing down -Z points where the clips aim.

**A stride's rate follows the planted foot.** The clips have no root motion, and they sweep the planted foot back unevenly (the walk's from standing still to nearly three metres a second at rate 1), so every constant rate skates it. For the walk, run and crouch walk the figure measures, once per clip, how far the lower foot travels back at each of 48 steps through it (`DotHumanoidStride.table_for`, from the tracks, in the figure's frame), and each frame plays as much clip as moves that foot back exactly as far as the body went (`_matched_rate`, capped at 4x). `ual_sprint` is a stride clip too: a runner the game marks `sprinting` (or, with no such key, above `sprint_speed`, 1.6 times the run speed when 0) plays it instead of the run, whose step covers about 1 m and popped a bone past 135 degrees at 15 fps from 4.5 m/s (141 to 146; with the sprint 125 at 7.5 m/s, 110 at 9). Its lowest foot still dips 5 to 6 mm past arena's sink limit at 7.5 and 9 m/s (clip grounding). A cut between stride clips keeps the feet planted but would snap the arms and the hand-held gun with them, so for `DotHumanoidBlend.SECONDS` (0.15 s) the upper body, hips and hand bone ease from the pose on screen into the new clip's (`DotHumanoidBlend.step`); `people_selftest` flickers the pace across the run and sprint thresholds and bounds the hand's step. Any other clip keeps the fixed divisors in `_clip_for`. A game whose walk is near the stock run threshold (4.0 m/s) calls `set_run_speeds`, or its walk plays the run clip.

**A hat or a crest goes on the crown.** `attachment("head_top")`, `"crest"` or `"hat"` is one mount on the head bone at the top of the mesh, upright and at metre scale, so it nods with the head and shrinks with it in first person (`hide_head`).

## What a crowd costs

- One surface and one material per look; the bodies are decimated to 42% at build (2.6 to 3.7 thousand triangles; 55% was tried first and cost a fifth more for no difference anybody could see in the looks sheet) and Godot's generated LODs take them down with distance (`lod_bias` 0.4); nothing drawn past 140 m.
- **No shadow past 28 m** (`SHADOW_RANGE`). The shadow pass drew every person at full detail and was more than half of a crowd's triangles: thirty people measured 267 thousand primitives with every shadow and 110 thousand with none.
- The clip advanced every frame within 22 m of the camera, every second frame to 45 m, every fourth beyond, with the skipped time carried; out of the camera's view (a cone round the diagonal, sphere of the height) or hidden, twice as far apart (`OFFSCREEN_STEP`), and redrawn the frame it comes back. Only a shadow and a kill-cam recording see an offscreen pose; hit zones are scaled from the height, not bones.
- Measured with `tools/people_sheet.gd --crowd=30` (thirty people running 6 to 30 m from the camera, each a different look) on the dev VM's software renderer (llvmpipe, a worst case, not a browser): 64 draw calls, 174 thousand primitives, about 50 ms a frame at 1280x720 in both renderers, 15 to 18 µs of script per person per frame; sixty people: 124 draw calls.
- **In a browser** (2026-10-12): `tools/crowd_bench.tscn` as the main scene of a scratch web export (no threads, compatibility renderer), in headless Chromium 150 with WebGL2 on SwiftShader (the VM has no GPU, so this is software rendering again, a worst case): thirty people at 1280x720, 76 draw calls, 190 thousand primitives, about 220 ms a frame, 42 µs of script per person per frame (the WebAssembly build is between two and three times the native one); with four of them fallen as skeletal ragdolls, 68 draw calls, 169 thousand primitives, 174 ms. The draw calls and primitives are what a GPU would get; the frame time is not. **On a real GPU** (2026-10-20, the same scratch export in Chrome 155 with WebGL2 through ANGLE on a GTX 1660 Ti, headless, 1280x720, driven over DevTools by `gpu.sh cdp` with the page served from the dev VM): thirty people 76 draw calls, 190 thousand primitives, 16.7 ms a frame (worst 20.6; that is the 60 Hz cap, so there is headroom the number does not show), 59 µs of script per person; with four skeletal ragdolls falling 75 draw calls, 193 thousand primitives, 16.6 ms (worst 18.2). Eighty zombie-zone commons with four dying: 81 draw calls, 194 thousand primitives, 16.7 ms, 47 µs of script per zombie. So the people are script-bound in a browser (about 1.8 ms a frame for thirty), not GPU-bound, and the 42% decimation and LOD bias stand. Frame time was not measured beyond the 60 Hz cap.
- **Carried figures** (`auto_animate`): `tools/npc_bench.tscn` (eight unarmed people 4 to 10 m away, moved at 128 ticks a second, frames at 355) costs 30 µs of script per person per frame, posed 118 times a second; it was 147 µs with 218 clip switches a second each before the measure spanned the ticks and the pose followed the steps (2026-11-12). At 60 frames a second every frame is posed, as before. `legs.on_floor` keeps its bones and chains per skeleton (it was 30 µs a pose of name lookups). `tools/anim_bench.tscn`'s `ANIM_DIGEST` is unchanged by both.
- Animation is client side only; a server never builds a figure.

## The ragdoll, and a critical

A dead person falls by its bones through dot-player-char's own ragdoll, not a second one: see [ragdolls.md](ragdolls.md#a-skinned-body). A critical that takes a limb off bakes the limb out of the skin and shrinks its bones to the stump ([design.md](design.md#a-body-coming-apart-is-presentation-seeded-from-the-death)); `mend()` on respawn. `hide_head` shrinks the head bone for your own body in first person, where the camera is inside it.

## A pose kept for a replay

A replay that only knows where a body stood and how fast it moved cannot redraw it: animated by speed inside a replay frame, with no time passing, the body stands frozen in whatever clip it last held. `pose_record()` returns the pose as drawn (the bone count, the model's transform, every bone's rotation, the root and hip positions; overlays, aim and the swim tilt included) as a `PackedFloat32Array`, and `pose_apply(a, b, alpha)` draws the blend of two of them on a body of the same skeleton (false and nothing changed otherwise). It is 203 floats (812 bytes) for the people's 46-bone skeleton; dot-replay's `DotKillcamHost.pose_fn` keeps them at `mp_killview_pose_hz`. `people_selftest` checks a run, crouch and aim sequence recorded on one figure and applied to another is the live pose bone for bone.

## Looking at them

`tools/people_sheet.gd` (under `xvfb-run`, not `--headless`) renders every clip of every body at six moments (default), a sheet of stock and site looks (`--looks`), a skinned body falling with each reaction (`--ragdoll`) and a critical taking a limb and everything off (`--break`); add `--rendering-method gl_compatibility` to see what a browser sees. Every made clip and every number above came from looking at those.

**A game newly linking this addon re-imports before rendering.** mg-look-at-me's first `--import` after the link registered the classes but left `people/` unimported: the figure fell back to its capsule (with a `player.humanoid` warning) and every suite passed. A second `--import` imported the bodies.

## Sprinting and the support hand

`set_motion` reads `"sprinting"`; `sprint_blend` eases over `SPRINT_SECONDS` and `_apply_aim` drops the gun arm by `SPRINT_DROP`. `set_support_grip(global_transform)` makes `_apply_support` reach the left hand there (two-bone IK, pole `SUPPORT_POLE`, weighted by `_support_weight`); a one-handed weapon passes none. `held_scale` is 1.7, times the weapon pose's world factor.

## Carrying a gun

Others see a held gun carried, not held out: `DotHumanoidCarryPose` (the figure's `carry`) writes the gun arm over the clip after the legs. Each `DotHumanoidCarry` class (`for_class`: rifle, smg, shotgun, sniper, heavy, pistol; null for melee or thrown) gives the gun hand's grip and the muzzle's turn for five stances: the low ready, a sprint across the body, the aim (shouldered, level with the view), a reload (pulled in and rolled to the other hand) and a swap (lowered). Between them it eases; it rides the chest's lean, sways and bobs with the stride (`walk_sway`/`run_sway`, damped when aiming), follows the view's pitch about the shoulder, and kicks on a shot.

- Until a game calls `carry.set_class(kind)` a gun is carried as a rifle with the clip's other hand; a class the game chose also puts the other hand on the foregrip (and along the magazine path while reloading). `set_class(null)` leaves the clip's arms alone.
- Per frame the game sets `carry.raised`, `carry.reloading` and `carry.switching`, and calls `carry.fired()` on a shot (shouldered for `AIM_HOLD` seconds). `sprint_blend` falls to 0 while the carry is drawn, so a weapon model that swings itself on a sprint does not do it twice.
- Drawn only: hit zones are scaled from the height, not bones, and the shot leaves from the view, so neither follows the carry. It runs on the clip's distance LOD frames only.
- Cost (`PEOPLE_CARRY=none|rifle tools/people_sheet.gd --crowd=32`, 2026-11-12, software renderer): 3 µs of script per person for the default carry, 7 µs with the foregrip hand (about 0.2 ms for 32 near people).
- `tools/people_sheet.gd --carry[=CLASS]` renders walk, run, sprint, fire, reload, swap and idle rows, side and front, with the carry off at the bottom for comparison.

## Your own arms holding a weapon

`DotPlayerViewArms` (under the camera's yaw pivot, API 8) draws the first-person hands on a weapon mesh the host supplies. A game calls, once, `arms.build(look, height)`, then each time the weapon changes `arms.set_held(DotViewGrip.one_hand(anchor, grip))` or `DotViewGrip.two_hand(anchor, grip, support)` (`anchor` is the weapon's node or a marker on it, the points are in its space, `Vector3.INF` for the support means one hand), and every frame `arms.tick(ground_speed, delta)` then `weapon_mount.position = rest_position + arms.sway()`. `arms.set_held(null)` puts it away. `arms.hand_error(grip)` (API 15) is the metres from each hand to its grip point plus `palm_offset`, -1 when not holding: for a test or a tool. While downed the crawl wins; `set_downed` is unchanged. Hide the backpack with `hide_vertex_fn`, as for the crawl.

The figure plays its held-gun clip and each arm is then reached onto the grip point by two-bone IK every tick, so the hands follow the weapon wherever the host moves it, sway or not. The kit has no wrist bone: the fingers hang off the forearm, so the hand's turn is the forearm's. The elbows bow out and down (`elbow_pole`), the eye sits `held_eye_from_head` from the head bone. The wrist sits `palm_offset` (default 0.015, -0.01, 0.02: on the grip's side, a little forward) from the grip point, mirrored for the support hand, so the drawn palm closes round the grip instead of hanging under it.

**The arms keep out of the gun** (`keep_arms_clear`, API 19). Each tick, after the IK, each holding arm tries elbow poles turned up to 80 degrees either way about its shoulder-to-grip line, scores how deep the upper arm and forearm (all but the hand end, `CLEAR_HAND_FREE`) go into the weapon's box grown by `arm_radius`, and eases toward the best one; `arm_depth_right` / `arm_depth_left` are what is left. The box is `DotViewGrip.bounds` in the anchor's space; left empty it is measured once from the visible meshes at and under the anchor, so a host whose anchor is a marker beside the mesh sets it. A long stock or a wide receiver still meets the forearm near the hand: the test rifle goes from 55 mm deep to 19.

**They are your own arms, dressed close up** (API 19). `build(look)` keeps the look as `own_look`; `set_look_override(look)` draws a server-chosen arm set instead (null gives your own back) and rebuilds only when the drawn look changes. With `detailed` (on by default) the mesh wears `DotViewArmsMaterial.for_look(look)`: the crowd's palette swap, plus mottled skin with a sheen and light through it, and a woven, worn cloth bump, read from each vertex's rest position (CUSTOM0), so the pattern does not swim as the arms bend. A flat swatch filling a quarter of the screen read as white plastic.

Look at `tools/view_arms_sheet.gd -- --held` (one hand, two hands, from the side, looking 60 degrees down; idle then walking) after changing any of it.

**In a browser** (2026-11-12, nightly web-view-arms-white): `tools/view_arms_web.tscn` as the main scene of a scratch web export (no threads) shows the crowd material, `DotViewArmsMaterial` with and without CUSTOM0, the crowd material with CUSTOM0 and a `StandardMaterial3D` on the atlas side by side. All five are textured in Chrome on SwiftShader and on an RTX 2070 (ANGLE, OpenGL ES 3.2). So when a game shows the arms white in a browser and its gun black on the same view layer, look at the game's view-layer light and setup first, not at these materials.

## Your own legs and body when you look down

`DotHumanoidFigure.first_person` (API 12) is for the local player's own figure seen from inside the head: `DotHumanoidOwnView.LEGS` shrinks everything from the belly up (the bone scale, as a critical does), `BODY` only the head, neck and arms (the view model draws the arms), `THIRD` puts them back. A shrunk mesh would cast a shrunk shadow, so in first person the figure's own mesh casts none and a shadow-only copy beside it (`OwnShadow`, the same mesh and skin on a second skeleton) takes every bone's pose each frame at full size. A head a critical took off stays off: the view puts back only the bones it shrank. The game places the figure (the feet on the floor under the eye) and picks the mode. Looking down, the figure trails its own body back behind the eye by itself (`own_look_down_back`, 1 m at 1.8 m, from 40 to 85 degrees down, read from `aim` before its clamp): straight over the cut waist, the hip cap fills the screen and reads as a face. A game that already pulls the body back sets it to 0; dot-player-controller's `mp_fp_body` is the server's default and a player's own setting overrides it.

The legs follow the way the body moves rather than the way it faces (`DotHumanoidLegs`, `legs` on the figure), from the `velocity` motion key in world space. Moving sideways, the root turns toward the motion up to 80 degrees and the hips are turned back as far, so the chest still faces the aim. Past 105 degrees from the facing (back under 75) the stride clip plays in reverse at the planted-foot rate, so a backward walk is a walk, not a forward one facing away. In a wall run the `wall` key (a world `Plane` facing the runner) puts each foot on the wall by two-bone IK, the higher foot of the stride kept off it by part of its clip height; a foot the leg cannot reach straight across is moved up the wall until it can, and the far one of a runner who is not rolled toward the wall stays short of it. The turn is written after the clip and taken off again before the next one advances, because the eased cut (`DotHumanoidBlend`) captures the hips. On the ground the feet are kept out of the floor (`DotHumanoidLegs.on_floor`): the gun walks and runs carry the drawn ankle (the end of the leg; the `Foot` bones are IK controls the mesh does not follow) under its rest height in 13 to 31 percent of frames, up to 0.07 m, at any body height, and the turned hips and far figures' skipped frames add more in a game. Each such leg is bent by two-bone IK until the ankle is back at its rest height in the figure's frame; it is a drawing pass after the clip like the turn, taken off with it, and skipped in the air, on a wall, a ladder, a seat, swimming, dead or downed. `tools/clip_metrics.gd -- --height 1.372 --body shaun` prints each stance's lowest sole and its share of frames under -1 cm. `people_selftest` checks that the planted foot stays put walking forward, backward and sideways, the clip's direction, the hips' turn, the wall-side foot on the wall and what each mode shrinks.

The whole body is stood on the floor under its origin too (`floor_mask`, `floor_reach`, `floor_lift` on the figure; `DotHumanoidLegs.floor_lift`). A game draws a body at a point interpolated between two ticks, and between a tick on the lower tread and one on the upper the point has the upper tread's x and z and a height in between: it is inside the riser, with the model sunk into the step by up to the step height (0.27 m measured in game-fatal-contact, 0.0 to 0.3 percent of frames on steps and slope crests; the same cut happens over a crest). Every frame the origin has moved (mid-hop too: a body crossing a step is often drawn in its jump or fall clip; and from `_process`, so a body a game animates only every few frames is still lifted), unless it swims, climbs, sits, drives or is dead, one ray goes down from `floor_reach` (0.45 m) above the origin to it, onto `floor_mask` (1, the shooter layout's `world`; 0 turns it off), and a floor face it meets (normal y at least 0.7) raises the model by the gap, at once and never eased (an eased rise is a body sunk into the step it just climbed), and the feet's floor with it. Drawing only: the origin, the hull, the hit zones and anything a game simulates stay where they were; a body above the floor (going down a step) is left floating, which reads far better than one in it. `people_selftest` checks the lift, its reach, the air, the mask, a hop and a move without an `animate` call.

`DotPlayerViewArms.forearm_scale` (default 1) shortens or lengthens the forearms while a gun is held; the fingers are divided by it so the hand keeps its size. A weapon row sets it with the `forearm_scale` art key (`zee-dot-weapons`), and the game scales every weapon with a cvar.

`DotPlayerViewArms.max_lean` (default 0.4, 0 is no lean) caps how far the arms lean toward a hand target out of their reach. A game sets it per weapon from the `max_lean` art key (`zee-dot-weapons`, below 0 keeps this default) and for all weapons with a cvar (`cl_fp_max_lean` in the reference game). A drawn lean that must shrink eases down at `LEAN_RATE` (1.2 m/s); one that must grow is taken at once, because a lagging lean left the support hand short of a two-hand grip drawn after a one-hand one (grenade_launcher draw, 0.26 m off for 4 frames). And a weapon swap does not refit the arms (only a change between holding, crawling and nothing does): a swap from a two-hand grip far out to a grenade's one-hand grip moved the shoulder 0.27 m in one frame, and the elbow jumped with it while the hand stayed on the grip.

`tools/clip_metrics.gd` measures foot skate, head pop and head jerk for idle, walk, run, the arena walk (4.05 m/s), sprint (9 m/s), crouch, crouch walk and slide, plus the biggest bone turn over 1/15 s (the filmstrip bone_pop gate, 135 degrees), and prints FLAG over the limits (`godot --headless --fixed-fps 60 --path . --script res://tools/clip_metrics.gd`). The crouch is bent 1.4 times the kit's duck and the crouch walk 1.0 (`CROUCH_IDLE_BEND`, `CROUCH_WALK_BEND`), so it reads visibly lower than standing.
